What it does — and who it is for

Most Mac tools record either your microphone or your screen — getting both your voice and the sound playing on your Mac into one clean file usually means virtual audio drivers, aggregate devices, and post-production syncing. AudioRecord does it directly: one click records the microphone and all system audio, mixed into a single WAV or M4A file.

Every recording lands in a searchable library with folders, color tags, and favorites. Rename, reorganize, and move files between folders — the library follows. Playback is instant, inside the app.

Built for people who record real things:

  • Students — tag lectures by course, find any class in seconds
  • Podcasters — capture interviews from any app, keep episodes organized
  • Professionals — archive online meetings with your commentary baked in
  • Anyone — voice memos that actually stay organized

Why "mixed into one file" matters

The usual way to record system audio on a Mac

macOS gives you the microphone easily, but internal audio — a Zoom call, a YouTube video, a Teams meeting — normally requires routing tools like virtual audio devices, or a separate screen recording you then strip audio from. Then you sync your mic track against it in an editor. It works; it is just not something you want to do every week.

One file, ready as recorded

AudioRecord captures both sources through Apple's ScreenCaptureKit and writes a single mixed file — with live balance control so the mix is right before you finish. Nothing to route, nothing to sync afterward.

Honest about the permission

Because system-audio capture uses ScreenCaptureKit, macOS asks once for Screen & System Audio Recording permission. The app walks you through it. That is the entire setup.

A recorder, not a subscription farm

No account, no cloud, no upload. The App Store privacy label says Data Not Collected. Recordings stay in your library, on your Mac.

Frequently asked questions

How do I record system audio on a Mac?
With AudioRecord: press ⌘⇧R, grant the one-time Screen & System Audio Recording permission macOS requests (the app walks you through it), and record. Everything playing on your Mac — Zoom, Meet, Teams, YouTube — is captured together with your microphone, mixed into one file. No virtual audio devices to configure.
Can it record a Zoom or Teams meeting with my voice on top?
Yes — that is the core use case. The meeting's audio (system audio) and your commentary (microphone) are recorded into a single file, and you can balance the two levels live while recording.
Which file formats does it save?
WAV (lossless) or M4A — chosen per recording, in the app.
Does it record internal audio only, without the microphone?
It is built around capturing both sources together, with live balance control — you can favor one source heavily when you mostly need the other. Balance each level live while recording.
Does any of my audio leave my Mac?
No. There is no account, no cloud service, and no upload. The App Store privacy label lists Data Not Collected. See the privacy policy for details.
What does it cost?
The download is free, and Full Version is a one-time $8.99 in-app purchase.
What are the requirements?
A Mac running macOS 14.0 or later. The app is under 1 MB; system-audio capture uses Apple's ScreenCaptureKit, which triggers the one-time permission request.
Where do I get it?
On the Mac App Store: AudioRecord: Mic + System.
