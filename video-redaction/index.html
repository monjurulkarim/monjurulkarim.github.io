How it works

The workflow is always the same four steps: import → mark what to redact → review → export. Here is each one in detail.

1

Import a video

Click “Choose a video file…” and pick any common video file. The app reads its details (duration, dimensions, frame rate, codecs, audio) under Source details — nothing is decoded or converted yet. The soundtrack is kept in exports (re-encoded) and is never analyzed; redaction touches only the picture.

2

Mark what to redact — automatically

In Automatic detection, check Face and/or License plate, then click Analyze entire video. The app proposes a track for every subject it finds — one subject followed through the video, with a box around it in every frame it appears. Detection only proposes: you review everything before any export. If you only want part of the video scanned, mark a segment with I / O and analyze just that range.

3

…or draw boxes yourself

For anything the detectors don't cover — a logo, a tattoo, a missed face — click ✚ Draw redaction box and drag a box around the subject at the playhead. The box then follows the subject automatically to the end of the video: you draw once. Drag to move, drag a corner to resize, and adjust each redaction's time range on the timeline (drag its edges, or ✕ to remove it). If tracking ever drifts, fix the box at that moment and use Re-track from the playhead.

4

Review and approve

Every track starts as “pending” and nothing is redacted until you decide: Approve it into the export, or Exempt (🛡) to leave that subject visible on purpose. ✓ Approve all confirms everything in one click; Approve confident approves only the detector's high-confidence findings and leaves the rest for you. Tracks flagged ⚠ had uncertain moments (occlusions, gaps where the box was interpolated or held) — check those spots, accept the span with X if the box looks right, or fix and re-track (U reopens an accepted span). When in doubt, approve: over-redacting is safer than missing a subject.

5

Choose the style

Per track in Track details: Black (solid black — strongest), Blur, or Pixelate; rectangle or oval shape (faces default to oval); margin padding; soft edge / feather. Black Safety is the one-click strongest preset — opaque black, hard rectangle, no feather, extra margin. Blur and pixelate keep the video watchable but are not guaranteed anonymization; black is.

6

Export & validate

In Export, click Export & validate and choose a save location. Export unlocks when every track is approved or exempt (and at least one is approved). The finished file is validated automatically — the app checks that every approved redaction is really present in every expected frame, and confirms ✓ Export validated only when it passes. If a check fails, no partial file is kept. Cancel mid-export and nothing partial is written.

What stays on your machine

Everything, by design

The video, the detection models, and the export all live on your disk. The app makes no network connections — you can verify with your firewall, or by going offline entirely: every feature still works. This page is the only thing your browser loads; the app itself is a normal desktop program.

Clean temporary data

Review thumbnails and other working data are stored under randomized, opaque names in your system temp folder and are deleted when you close the app or switch videos. The in-app log drawer shows <source> / <output> instead of your real file paths.

Troubleshooting

“Worker connection lost” / an offline banner appears
The app's analysis engine is a separate process; if it ever dies, the app restarts it automatically. If a banner persists, quit and relaunch the app, then run the job again.
Export is disabled
The button lists how many tracks are still pending. Approve or exempt every track (and have at least one approved), and the export unlocks.
A face or plate wasn't detected
Try a smaller detection interval, make sure the category is checked — or just draw a box over it. Manual boxes track the subject exactly like detections do.
Plate detection finds nothing
The plate model needs real, legible plates. Tiny, angled, or motion-blurred plates may need a manual box.

Still stuck? Write to support@mkarim.us.

Licenses & open-source

Video Redactor runs entirely on your computer and builds on excellent open-source software. The complete third-party notices and component list ship inside the app (THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md) and are included with every download. The source offers below are maintained for at least three years from release.

FFmpeg (GPL-3.0-or-later)

Each download bundles a private, unmodified FFmpeg build so the app never depends on a system install: the macOS app ships FFmpeg 7.1.1 (Homebrew build); the Windows installer ships FFmpeg 8.1.2 (gyan.dev “full” build, statically linked). Both builds enable GPL components and are distributed under GPL-3.0-or-later. Video Redactor launches FFmpeg as a separate, unmodified external command and is not a derivative work of it.

  • FFmpeg source for the shipped versions: ffmpeg.org (tags n7.1.1 and n8.1.2)
  • macOS build recipe (complete build script): the public Homebrew formula ffmpeg 7.1.1_2
  • Windows build origin: gyan.dev FFmpeg builds — unmodified release binaries produced by Gyan Doshi; the full configure line for the shipped build is recorded in the bundled THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md

YuNet face detector (MIT)

The face model (face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx) is the YuNet 2023 model from the OpenCV Zoo, distributed under the MIT License by its author within the Apache-2.0 OpenCV project. Model source.

YOLO11n license-plate detector (AGPL-3.0)

The plate model (license-plate-finetune-v1n.onnx) is a YOLO11-nano fine-tune by morsetechlab, trained on the Roboflow license-plate-recognition-rxg4e dataset (CC BY 4.0), distributed under AGPL-3.0. It ships unmodified inside the app. Model source · Ultralytics YOLO11 (AGPL-3.0) · Training dataset (CC BY 4.0).

The full license texts (GPL-3.0, AGPL-3.0, MIT) and the complete component inventory (Python, npm, and Rust dependencies) are in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md, included with every download. Video Redactor itself is proprietary software; all listed third-party components are used under their respective licenses, and none of them communicates over the network at runtime.
